The first element of BCP (see [[BCP vs DRP]]) sets up the effort before any analysis happens — defining who is involved, what's in scope, and what constraints apply.

- **Organizational review** — understand the organization's structure, critical departments, and how they interrelate, so the plan reflects how the business actually operates
- **BCP team selection** — a cross-functional team with representatives from business units, IT, security, legal, HR, and facilities; needs executive sponsorship to have authority and get resourced
- **Resource requirements** — budget, staff time, and tools needed to conduct the BIA and build the plan
- **External requirements** — obligations imposed from outside the organization that shape scope and priorities:
	- **Vendors** — dependencies on third parties and their own continuity posture
	- **Legal and regulatory requirements** — industry regulations, contractual SLAs, and legal obligations that may mandate certain continuity capabilities or reporting

Executive sponsorship isn't just practical — officers and directors have a fiduciary duty of **due diligence** to ensure adequate continuity planning is in place. Board-level review of the BCP is how that obligation gets satisfied.
