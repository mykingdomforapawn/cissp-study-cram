The final element of BCP (see [[BCP vs DRP]]) turns the plan from a document into an organizational capability. Throughout every element, the safety of human life is the paramount concern — above facilities, infrastructure, or financial loss — and this priority should be explicit in whatever is disseminated to employees.

- **Senior management approval** — the plan needs formal sign-off to carry authority, secure funding, and confirm it aligns with organizational priorities
- **Implementation** — rolling the plan out: assigning ownership, distributing procedures, and building awareness/training so staff know their role during a disruption
- **Maintenance** — the plan is a living document, updated when triggered by organizational change (new systems, reorgs, mergers), changes in risk, or on a periodic review schedule
- **Testing** — validates the plan actually works, in increasing order of rigor and disruption:
	- **Checklist** — participants review the plan document for completeness/accuracy
	- **Tabletop / structured walkthrough** — team talks through a scenario step by step without taking real action
	- **Simulation** — a realistic scenario is role-played, still without disrupting live operations
	- **Parallel** — recovery systems are actually activated alongside production, which keeps running
	- **Full interruption** — production is actually taken down and recovery is executed for real; highest confidence, highest risk
