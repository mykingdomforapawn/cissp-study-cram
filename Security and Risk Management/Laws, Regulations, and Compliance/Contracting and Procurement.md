Security requirements need to be built into the acquisition process itself, not bolted on after a vendor or product is already selected — by the time a contract is signed, leverage to demand security concessions is largely gone.

- **RFP/RFQ stage** — security requirements (data handling, certifications, right-to-audit) are stated as evaluation criteria alongside cost and functionality, so vendors are compared on security posture too
- **Contract terms** — SLAs, breach notification timelines, and audit rights get written into the contract itself, making them enforceable rather than assumed

For the detailed agreement types (MSA, SoW, NDA) and ongoing third-party lifecycle management, see [[Vendor, Consultant, and Contractor Agreements]] — this note is specifically about baking security into the acquisition decision, not managing the relationship afterward.
