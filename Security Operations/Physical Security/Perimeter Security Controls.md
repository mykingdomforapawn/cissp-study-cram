The perimeter is the outer layer of physical defense (see [[Defense in Depth]]). Its controls **deter** casual intrusion, **deny** easy access, and **delay** determined intruders. A barrier nobody watches only delays, so detection and response must follow.

## Fences and Gates

| Height | Effect |
|---|---|
| **3-4 ft** | Deters casual trespassers only |
| **6-7 ft** | Hard to climb casually |
| **8 ft plus barbed wire** | Deters determined intruders (high security) |

- **Construction** matters: tight mesh (no footholds), anchoring below ground, anti-climb and anti-cut materials, and **barbed or razor wire**. **PIDAS** sensors detect vibration or cutting.
- **Gates** are the planned weak point, so they must be as strong as the fence, limited in number, and controlled (guards, locks, alarms). Gate classes I to IV range from residential to restricted industrial.
- **Bollards** stop **vehicles** reaching buildings or walkways. Barricades and berms do similar work.

## Turnstiles and Person Traps

- **Turnstiles** pass people **one at a time**, in one direction, controlling flow. Short ones can be climbed over.
- **Person traps (mantraps)** are a small space with **two interlocked doors**: the first must close and lock before the second opens, holding the person until verified. This prevents **tailgating and piggybacking** (see [[Physical Access Controls]]). Weight sensors or cameras can confirm only one person entered. Standard at data center entrances, with a safe emergency release.

## Lighting

Lighting is cheap and effective. It **deters** intruders, enables **detection** by guards and cameras, and aids safety.

- Cover perimeter, entrances, parking, and walkways with **overlapping coverage** and no dark spots. Avoid glare that blinds guards or cameras.
- Brightness is measured in **foot-candles** or lux. Cameras need compatible lighting or infrared.
- Types: **continuous**, **standby** (on trigger), and **emergency** (backup power, supporting evacuation).
- Lighting is the **most commonly deployed** physical mechanism, but only a weak deterrent. It should not be the sole control except at low threat levels, and it works best with cameras and guards.
- Compare: **fences** are preventive, **CCTV** is detective, **guards** are the most flexible, and **key locks** are the most common and cheapest access control device, inside and out.

## Security Guards

The most **flexible** control. Guards observe, judge, respond, and adapt, serving as deterrent and response.

- **Strengths:** judgment, verifying alarms, challenging people, and flexible response.
- **Weaknesses:** **cost**, fatigue, and exposure to **bribery, collusion, or social engineering**. Prescreening, **bonding**, and training do not guarantee a reliable guard. Not every environment suits guards. Guards usually do not know the scope of operations inside, which limits how well they can respond but also **protects confidentiality**.
- **Management:** clear post orders, **background checks**, training, rounds and incident logs, and supervision.
- **In-house** guards give more control at higher cost. **Contracted** guards are cheaper but less controlled, so vet the vendor.
- Guards must know the limits of their authority and use of force.

## Guard Dogs

Dogs **deter and detect** with hearing and smell, and can apprehend intruders. They suit open areas, industrial sites, and warehouses at night.

- They need **handlers**, training, and care, and carry **liability** if they injure someone.
- They cannot tell authorized from unauthorized people without a handler, so they should not be a sole control.
- Keep them away from public areas and emergency exits (life safety).

## Key Points

- **Fence height:** 3-4 ft deters casual trespass, **6-7 ft** most people, **8 ft plus wire** determined intruders.
- **Gates** are the weak point, **bollards** stop vehicles, **turnstiles** control one-way flow.
- A **person trap** (two interlocked doors) prevents tailgating and holds people until verified.
- **Lighting** deters and aids detection but is not a barrier. **Emergency lighting** supports evacuation.
- **Guards** are the most flexible but costly and fallible. **Dogs** need handlers and bring liability.
