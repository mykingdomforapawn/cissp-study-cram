Controls protect only if people are kept safe, rules are followed, and results are measured. This note covers **life safety**, **regulatory requirements**, and **key performance indicators**.

## Life Safety and Environmental Issues

**People come first.** When protecting assets conflicts with protecting people, safety wins, in control design as much as in emergencies.

- **Egress:** exits are **unobstructed**, marked, lit, and open outward or fail open where required. A door that traps people is a life safety failure (see **fail-safe vs fail-secure** in [[Facility Planning and Design]]).
- **Emergency preparedness:** an **occupant emergency plan (OEP)** covers evacuation routes, assembly points, **roll call** (including visitors), and floor wardens, and is practiced with **drills**. It differs from business continuity, which restores operations (see [[BCP vs DRP]]).
- **Emergency systems:** emergency lighting and signs on backup power, fire alarms, first aid and AEDs, and protected **emergency power off** switches.
- **Environmental hazards:** hazardous materials, gas suppression (people must be able to leave first: see [[Fire Protection]]), air quality, and earthquakes, floods, storms, and extreme heat, with shelter-in-place plans as well as evacuation.
- **Staff safety:** duress alarms, escort and lone-worker policies, safe parking and lighting, workplace-violence procedures, and travel safety (see [[Personnel Security Lifecycle]]).
- **Duty of care** is the organization's responsibility to protect people, and neglecting it creates liability (see [[Risk Management Concepts]]).

## Regulatory Requirements

Laws, codes, and standards vary by location and industry, and compliance is a baseline.

- **Building and fire codes** set rules for exits, sprinklers, alarms, fire ratings, and occupancy. Fire marshals and inspectors enforce them, and changes need permits.
- **Accessibility laws** (such as the **ADA**) require that doors, turnstiles, and intercoms be usable by people with disabilities.
- **Workplace safety rules** (such as **OSHA**).
- **Industry standards** such as **PCI DSS** and **HIPAA** require physical controls. Auditors check access, visitor logs, and camera coverage.
- **Privacy and labor laws** limit surveillance and how monitoring data is kept and shared (see [[Laws]]).
- **Environmental rules** on suppression agents (Montreal Protocol) and generator fuel storage.
- **Insurers** often require alarm and suppression systems.
- Rules vary by **jurisdiction** (see [[Data Localization and Sovereignty]]), so compliance is documented and reviewed on a schedule.

## Key Performance Indicators

**KPIs** are measurable values showing whether physical security works and improves. They are tied to objectives, tracked over time, and reported to management (see [[Organizational Security Processes]]).

| Area | Example indicators |
|---|---|
| **Incidents** | Number, type, trend, and cost (theft, breaches, tailgating) |
| **Detection and response** | **Time to detect, respond, resolve**, and verified vs **false alarms** |
| **Access control** | Unauthorized attempts, forced or held doors, badge audit results, time to **revoke** access |
| **Coverage** | Camera and sensor uptime, blind spots |
| **Testing and drills** | Physical **penetration test** results, alarm tests, evacuation times |
| **Compliance** | Open audit findings, inspection results, time to remediate |
| **Maintenance** | Equipment inspected on schedule, overdue maintenance |
| **People** | Training completion, awareness test results |

- Good KPIs are **specific, measurable, relevant, and goal-tied**. Outcomes (time to detect and respond) beat easy counts (badge swipes).
- Findings feed **lessons learned** and control changes, with periodic assessment of the whole program (see [[Risk Assessment]]).
- Use a defined method and independent review, since pressure for good numbers can hide problems.

## Key Points

- **Life safety first:** exits open and unobstructed, doors fail open where people could be trapped, emergency lighting and drills.
- **OEPs** protect people, **business continuity plans** restore operations.
- Meet **building and fire codes, accessibility laws, safety rules, industry standards**. They vary by jurisdiction. **Privacy** law limits surveillance.
- **KPIs** (incidents, **time to detect and respond**, false alarms, access audits, uptime, drill and test results) show effectiveness and drive improvement.
