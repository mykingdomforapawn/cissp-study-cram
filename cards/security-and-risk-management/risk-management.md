## Card 1

**Domain**
1 — Security and Risk Management / Risk Management

**Question**
A manufacturing company identifies a risk of prolonged production downtime following a ransomware incident. To treat it, management purchases a cyber insurance policy covering breach response, legal liability, and business interruption. During the next review cycle, the risk manager notes that residual risk for this scenario still exceeds the organization's documented risk appetite. What should the risk manager do <b>FIRST</b>?

**Options**
*Revisit the treatment decision and apply additional controls or select a different response — residual risk above appetite means the treatment is incomplete and the decision must be reworked.
Have the risk owner formally document acceptance of the residual risk — acceptance is only a valid response when residual risk falls within appetite.
Increase the coverage limits on the cyber insurance policy — transfer reduces financial exposure only; it changes neither likelihood nor impact, so residual risk is unaffected.
Recommend raising the organization's risk appetite to match the residual risk — appetite is a governance decision reflecting business objectives, not a lever for closing a control gap.

## Card 2

1 — Security and Risk Management / Risk Management

**Question**
A company purchases cyber insurance covering ransomware, and the CFO states in a board update that the ransomware risk has now been reduced. The risk manager disagrees. Which statement is <b>MOST</b> accurate?

**Options**
*Insurance transfers the financial consequence but leaves likelihood and operational impact unchanged — the risk itself has not been reduced.
Insurance is a form of risk avoidance, because the loss no longer falls on the organization — avoidance means eliminating the activity that creates the risk, which has not happened.
Insurance constitutes formal risk acceptance once the policy is signed — acceptance means taking no action; buying cover is a transfer decision.
Insurance is a compensating control substituting for technical safeguards — compensating controls are security controls, not financial instruments.

## Card 3

**Domain**
1 — Security and Risk Management / Risk Management

**Question**
A bank's board states that it is willing to accept a defined level of operational risk in pursuit of growth, and separately notes the maximum loss the institution could absorb before its solvency would be threatened. Which two concepts are these, in that order?

**Options**
*Risk appetite, then risk capacity — appetite is willingness to take risk; capacity is the maximum the organization can absorb before viability is threatened.
Risk tolerance, then risk limit — tolerance is the acceptable deviation around appetite, and a limit is a hard boundary triggering escalation.
Risk appetite, then risk tolerance — tolerance describes permitted variance from appetite, not the absolute survivable maximum.
Risk capacity, then risk appetite — the two are correctly named but stated in the wrong order.

**Question**
A business unit decides not to remediate a moderate risk, because the cost of the control exceeds the expected loss. What must happen for this to be a valid risk response rather than simply ignoring the risk?

**Options**
*The acceptance is formally documented and assigned to an accountable owner — undocumented, unowned acceptance is indistinguishable from ignoring the risk.
The decision is approved by the internal audit function — audit independently validates controls and does not approve treatment decisions.
A compensating control is implemented to bring residual risk down — adding a control makes the response mitigation rather than acceptance.
The risk is removed from the risk register once accepted — accepted risks stay on the register for periodic review.

1 — Security and Risk Management / Risk Management

**Question**
A data centre is valued at 2,000,000. A flood would destroy an estimated 30 percent of that value, and historical records suggest such a flood occurs once every twenty years. What is the annualized loss expectancy?

**Options**
*30,000 — the single loss expectancy is 2,000,000 × 0.30 = 600,000, and the annualized figure is 600,000 × 0.05 = 30,000.
600,000 — this is the single loss expectancy, not yet annualized by the rate of occurrence.
100,000 — applies the twenty-year interval to the full asset value while ignoring the exposure factor.
12,000,000 — multiplies by the recurrence interval instead of dividing by it.

## Card 6

**Domain**
1 — Security and Risk Management / Risk Management

**Question**
A proposed control would reduce a risk's annualized loss expectancy from 250,000 to 40,000. The vendor licence costs 90,000 per year, and the security team estimates a further 70,000 annually in administration, training, and lost productivity. What should the recommendation be?

**Options**
*Implement it — the 210,000 annual saving exceeds the 160,000 total annual cost of the safeguard, giving a net value of 50,000.
Implement it — the 210,000 saving comfortably exceeds the 90,000 licence fee, so the value is 120,000. The decision is right but the annual cost of a safeguard includes administration, training, and productivity loss, not just the licence.
Reject it — at 160,000 the safeguard costs more than the 40,000 of loss that remains after it is deployed. The comparison should be against the reduction achieved, not the residual.
Reject it — a safeguard cannot be justified while the risk is not eliminated entirely. No control eliminates risk; the test is whether the reduction outweighs the cost.

A vendor announces three dates for a product: it will stop selling it, then stop offering technical support, and finally stop issuing security patches. From a risk perspective, which is the <b>MOST</b> significant threshold?

**Options**

End of life — the product is on a deprecation path, but updates may still be issued.
End of support — assistance ends, though security patches may continue to be released.
All three are equivalent, because the product is obsolete in each case — the three dates have materially different security consequences.
