A baseline (see the policy hierarchy in [[Security Governance & Management]]) defines the minimum security configuration a system must meet. Rather than writing one from scratch, organizations typically start from a published framework — most commonly NIST SP 800-53 (see [[Security Control Frameworks]]) — and adapt it to their own environment through two related processes:

- **Scoping** — determining which controls in the framework actually apply, based on the system's environment, technology, and risk profile; controls that don't apply (e.g., a control for a technology the organization doesn't use) are excluded
- **Tailoring** — adjusting the wording or strength of the controls that remain, to fit the organization's specific context (e.g., strengthening a password policy control beyond the framework's minimum because of higher risk tolerance requirements)

The result is an organization-specific baseline: still traceable back to the source framework for audit purposes, but scaled to what's actually relevant rather than applied as a generic checklist.
